# Silence, Sponsored by Spotify Premium

Every morning, my commute is sacred. 

I connect my AirPods, open Spotify (the free version), endure a 30-second ad about something I will never buy, and then drift peacefully into my playlist. It’s calming. It’s predictable. It’s enough. Premium, to me, has always felt unnecessary—why pay extra when I’ve already mastered the art of tolerating mild inconvenience?

But one morning, as I settled into my office shuttle, Spotify made a suggestion I did not ask for. A notification appeared: “Get 3 months of Spotify Premium FREE.”

Premium. Free. Three months.

Now, I had no intention of paying after those three months. None. Zero. But my brain whispered, “It’s free. Why not?” And just like that, my peaceful commute was sacrificed at the altar of a good deal.

I clicked the link. That’s when Spotify revealed the fine print: This offer is only for new accounts. My current account—loyal, ad-tolerant, hardworking—was suddenly worthless.

Instead of listening to music like a sane person, I chose chaos. I took out my work phone, installed Spotify, created a brand-new account with my work email, because clearly this was now a professional endeavor. Then I forwarded the offer email from my personal phone to my work email like I was conducting an undercover operation.

I opened the link. Opened my wallet. Found the card. Entered the card details. Entered the billing address. Verified the OTP. Answered a few existential questions about why I was doing this. At this point, I was deeply committed. Turning back was not an option.

Halfway through the setup—beep. One AirPod died.

The other stayed on. Barely. Bravely. I adjusted. Accepted one-ear music like a survivor. I kept setting up the account, silently praying. Please. Just last till this is done.

And then, finally—Spotify Premium was activated.

That’s when the second AirPod gave up. It shut down within seconds, taking my music, my peace, and the entire point of this exercise with it.

I sat there, holding a phone with Spotify Premium activated, and ears that could hear nothing. No music. No ads. Just the hum of the road and my own poor life choices echoing loudly in my head.
The rest of the ride, I stared into the distance like a character in an indie film—someone who had everything, yet nothing.

I had lost my peaceful commute. I had gained Spotify Premium.
I suffered through silence....so future me could skip ads.
