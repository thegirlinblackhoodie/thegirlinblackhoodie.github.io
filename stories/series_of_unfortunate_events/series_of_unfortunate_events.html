You ever have one of those days where the universe looks at you and says, "Let's make this one personal"? Yeah. I just had one of those days.

So I had a terrible day at work. Long, exhausting day — mostly me fixing other people's mess. Random bugs, prod deployments, load tests — basically, I was the janitor of the team. Because my manager — who, by the way, is not even an engineer — seems to think "ML Engineer" means every kind of engineer except the one that does ML. DevOps? That's me. Tech support? Still me. Backend, testing, deployments — all me. If there's a mess, it's magically MLE territory. I've become the engineering buffet — you name it, I serve it. Except the one dish I actually specialize in — apparently that's "too spicy for the project right now."


I moved countries. Spent thousands of dollars. Did a Master's in Artificial Intelligence. And now I'm doing the kind of work companies normally outsource. So basically, I just imported myself.



My day was already going bad and then I missed my shuttle home because of all the "urgent cleanups." I didn't wanna take a cab — I told myself it's because I needed some "alone time in the fresh air." But let's be honest… it's because I'm not rich enough to casually drop thirty dollars on a one-way Uber. My healing journey was just budget constraints with a scenic view. So I took the bus to Caltrain station. Reached there just in time… to watch the train leave. The next one? In 30 minutes. And I thought, "Okay, I don't need that much fresh air. I'm not healing. I'm just broke." So I took another bus — the right number bus. Sat down, looked out the window, and started accepting my fate. Like, "You know what? Maybe this is my story arc. Maybe this is character growth."


Twenty-five minutes later, I randomly decided to check Maps… and I realize I'm going in the wrong direction. I took the right bus — going the wrong way which pretty much sums up my career so far.


So I panic, get off at the next stop — and now there's no network at all! Zero bars. No signal. No hope. I'm standing there in the hot California sun, thinking, "Wow, this is how they'll find me on the news — holding a dead phone and unfulfilled dreams." My plan now? Cross the road. Wait at the bus stand. And pray the same bus does a full loop like karma coming back for me — because Lord knows karma owes me at this point. Fifteen minutes later — a bus came! Got in, sat down, and thought, "Maybe things are finally turning around."



Once I reached home, I texted my friend in New York, telling her the whole sob story. She felt bad for me — she's like, "I'll send you pastries to cheer you up!" And I'm like, "Wow, maybe the universe does care."


Thirty minutes later, she messages, "It says delivered!" But I hadn't received anything. I open the door — nothing. She sends a photo — and I notice the door number was right… but the building was wrong. So now I'm sprinting across the apartment complex like a desperate DoorDash detective. I get there — gone. Somebody took it.


And here's the dilemma — Do I knock? Do I fight a stranger for pastries I didn't even pay for? Like, "Excuse me, sir, have you seen my emotional stability in cupcake form?"


But then I thought — if they really wanted to return it, they would've. They had the address, the name, everything. So they looked at it and went, "Free sugar? Thank you, universe." I went home, angry-texted DoorDash customer care like they personally robbed me. "Hi, my pastries were stolen, and also… life's unfair." They refunded my friend, but the emotional damage? Still on a monthly plan.



I lay down thinking maybe tomorrow will be a better day. Yeah… and I'll finally do real ML work too, right? More like Alice in Wonderland.


Today wasn't just a bad day. It was a beta version of rock bottom — and knowing my luck, the full release drops next week.
