# A Freshly Cooked Disaster

I never really cooked when I lived back home in India. After moving to the US for grad school, I slowly learned from my roommate, and by the time I shifted to the Bay Area two years later, I was actually a decent cook. I could follow recipes, experiment a little, and even impress myself sometimes.

One day, after work, I made a big meal. It tasted amazing. I ate proudly… until midnight, when my stomach staged a full rebellion. The pain was so intense I cried. I took medicines, tossed and turned the whole night, and finally felt a little better by morning.

Since I had no idea what caused it, I assumed the safest thing would be to stick to home-cooked food. So I ate the same thing again. And—of course—the pain returned with even more commitment.

At this point, I really started doubting my cooking skills. But how could it be my food? Everything was fresh. Nothing smelled weird. Nothing looked suspicious. I had no answers. So I ran a scientific experiment: I ordered plain rice and dal from outside.

When the food came, I looked at it like it was an explosive device. What if my stomach was sensitive to any food now? But hunger won, and I ate it… and magically, I was completely fine. And that’s when I realized—it was my food. My lovingly cooked, beautifully seasoned enemy, plotting against me.

I threw everything out, bought fresh ingredients, and cooked again—confident, hopeful, innocent. And again, the same dramatic intestine-twisting pain hit me, like someone wringing out a wet towel inside me. 

Defeated, I finally gave up and decided to eat outside for a few days. And honestly...
From saying “I am sick, I will eat home-cooked meals for some days” to “I am sick, I am ordering dal-chawal from outside,” we all grew up.

Later, I texted the friend who taught me to cook and narrated the entire horror story. She replied instantly, almost too confidently: “Check your masalas. They’re probably expired.”

Until that moment, I had only considered vegetables and meat when I thought of “fresh ingredients.” It never once crossed my mind that masalas could betray me. But the giant packets, one person cooking—it all made sense.

Sure enough, several of the spices had expired just a few weeks earlier. I threw them out, replaced them, and like magic, everything went back to normal.
And that is the story of how I managed to poison myself… powered by expired masala and zero common sense.
