# Laughing Through the Pain (Literally)
Friday night, I was in horrible pain. I took medicines and cried almost the entire night. Around 4:30 a.m., I don’t even know if I slept or just passed out — my body just shut down.

I woke up at 8:30 a.m., looked at the time, and said, “Absolutely not,” and went back to sleep. Next time I opened my eyes, it was 11:30. I forced myself to get up, eat something, and take more painkillers.

I had a 4 p.m. stand-up comedy show booked. Honestly, I didn’t need to go. But I told myself: take a bath, cook some rice, eat, then decide. By 2:30 p.m., I was exhausted again. I set an alarm for 3:15, thinking I’d decide based on how I felt.

3:15 — I woke up. Still unsure.
3:30 — I was fully ready. Still unsure.
I booked a cab with the “wait” option, partly to save money, mostly to give myself more time to cancel. I even carried painkillers, telling myself, “It’s just 1.5 hours. You can survive.”

I reached the venue. Big crowd. My seat was Row 10, Seat 1. I thought, “Perfect. Seat 1 must be a corner. Easy escape if I feel worse.”

The usher looked at my ticket and said sweetly, “Oh honey, you’re seat 1 — right in the center! Best view in the house!”

Ma’am… I was looking for the worst view with the fastest exit.

I had to climb two floors because I couldn’t find the elevator. By the time I sat down, I was already tired again. It was 3:50. The show was at 4.

4:00 — no comedian.
4:20 — a random guy with a keyboard comes on stage and says he’ll sing “a couple of songs.”

“A couple” turned out to be 20 minutes.

My leg and hand started hurting again. I had painkillers but no water. I considered leaving, but now the theater was full and I was seated dead center. To exit, I would have had to disturb at least 30 people and become the main character of the balcony. I did not want that spotlight.

4:40 — singer finishes. Looks backstage. I pray this is it.
He says, “I’ll sing a couple more songs.”

Sir. No.

I whispered “no” under my breath, and three people around me turned and laughed. As if they weren’t suffering too.

5:00 — finally, the comedian arrives. People cheer. I had zero enthusiasm left. He started with crowd work. On a normal day, I would’ve laughed. But that day? I was sitting there thinking:

“You need to earn this laugh. I am not wasting my remaining energy on mid jokes.”

It was funny. I admit it.
But not funny enough for the pain I had climbed two floors for.

Later I found out he was an hour late to the next show too. So maybe this wasn’t a delay — maybe this was the schedule.

What was supposed to be a comedy show turned into an endurance test.

I went there to laugh.
Instead, I stress-tested my pain tolerance.
