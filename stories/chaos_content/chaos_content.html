I knew it was a bad idea the moment I did it. A weekday. I stayed up way too late and finally went to bed at 4:00 AM—fully aware that I’d somehow have to wake up around 7:30 AM, like I usually do without an alarm. My internal alarm clock has always been oddly reliable… just not officially certified.


Just three and a half hours later, I was expected to be a functioning adult catching the 8:08 AM shuttle to work. But at that moment, sleep felt more important than logic.


Morning came brutally. I woke up heavy-eyed, barely able to open them. Everything felt slow, foggy—exactly what you’d expect after barely any sleep. I squinted at my phone to check the time. The screen was blurry, but I could make out “8:00”


Panic. This was not the plan. I was supposed to be up 30 minutes earlier. Now I had exactly eight minutes to transform from barely conscious to office-ready.


I jumped out of bed —brushed my teeth, washed my face, combed my hair, threw on jeans and a T-shirt, packed my lunch, grabbed my bag. It was chaotic, rushed, but weirdly efficient.


While putting on my shoes, I checked the time again.


8:08AM. Perfect. I had done it. A new personal record. A new level unlocked: Bed to Badass in 8 minutes.


Feeling mildly impressed with myself, I checked the shuttle app to see how close it was. But… it wasn’t. The shuttle was nowhere near my stop. That didn’t make sense. I checked again—no delay notifications. Confused, I went back to my phone to recheck the time. 6:08.


I froze.


Somehow, in my half-asleep state, I had read 6:00 as 8:00 and launched into a full-blown morning sprint for absolutely no reason.


I didn’t know whether to be impressed or annoyed. On one hand, I had proven that even on two hours of sleep, I could function like a machine. On the other hand, I had just sacrificed precious sleep for nothing. But then I thought—well, at least this is blog-worthy. So, not a total loss.


I set a proper alarm for 8:00 AM, decided I’d just freshen up and leave since I was basically ready anyway, and collapsed back into bed. My eyes shut instantly. I was out.


When I woke up again, it was 7:55 AM. This time, I made sure my eyes were fully open before trusting anything. Just to be safe, I asked Siri. “7:55 AM PST,” she confirmed. Good. Reality check passed.


I reached for my phone to turn off my alarm—but there was no alarm. Strange. I got out of bed, still slightly confused.


And then I noticed. I wasn’t wearing jeans and a T-shirt. My hair was a mess. My work laptop was lying next to me on the bed. My bag was on the floor. Lunch? Not packed. Nothing was done. That’s when it hit me. None of it had happened.


The whole perfectly chaotic, impressively efficient 8-minute morning… was just a dream. And just like that, the panic returned—but this time, it was real. Because now, I actually had to do it. For real.


But somewhere in the middle of that very real panic, one thought quietly popped up— Wow. This story just got way more interesting.


I was less concerned about missing the shuttle and more impressed that my brain had managed to add a plot twist.


Blog-worthy level: upgraded.
