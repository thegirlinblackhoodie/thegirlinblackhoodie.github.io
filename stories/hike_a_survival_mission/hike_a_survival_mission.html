So one fine morning, I woke up and thought — let's be more active. Yeah, it was one of those motivational mornings where you're like, "New day, new me!"


My plan? Every Saturday morning I'd wake up early, go for a hike, feel good, lose weight — basically become that person who posts "morning hike + gratitude" stories.


I prepared seriously for this. I got myself an Adidas cap, a giant 3-litre water bottle with a strap (so I could wear it like a fashion statement), and Ray-Ban Meta AI glasses — for those amazing pictures of the amazing views. Not a paid promo, okay? Those glasses actually take crazy photos. For real.


The place I chose was 8 miles away. I don't drive, so I took a cab. I was all pumped up, fully motivated — you know, that gym-new-year-resolution energy.

When I reached the trail, it was so beautiful. I started the hike, took detours, clicked photos, feeling like a nature influencer who's also having a spiritual awakening.


Three hours later, I was back down. Then I roamed around the lake for two more hours — just walking, clicking pics. I saw this one bird sitting right in the middle of the lake. And my brain went: photo opportunity! So I zoomed in, zoomed out, tilted, crouched, stood, sat, lay down, flipped my phone upside down… The only thing left was doing a headstand. Which, honestly, I could've done, because my camera was in my glasses — my hands were free!


After this National Geographic moment, I decided to head home. Opened my phone — and boom — no network. Not "no internet," not "one bar." No. No network. Period. My heart sank. Slowly realized… I was stranded. So I started walking back toward the park entrance — a 45-minute panic walk — no footpath, just me, on the road, praying I don't become roadkill. Like, "Dear universe, I didn't wake up early to die flat on asphalt." Because if I'm dying, I want it to be dramatic — bungee jumping, paragliding, something cinematic! So people can at least say, "She lived her last moments to the fullest." And not become a news headline — "local girl dies trying to get signal."


Anyway, back to the story —

I finally reached the entrance. Asked the rangers for help. They were nice enough to let me use their Wi-Fi. Got a bit of signal — just enough to send messages, not enough to make calls though.


And then, I see a bunch of texts from my flatmate. At first, he's like, "Hey, where are you? When are you coming back?" Aww, sweet right? NO. Don't be so naïve. Nobody cares that much about me. I know it. I don't live in a fantasy world. Delulu is not my solulu! I scrolled down — and there it was. He wanted to go out with his friends… but he didn't have his keys. He wasn't worried about me, he was worried about his plans. And judging by the time, it had already been an hour since he texted — which meant he left the apartment unlocked. So now, not only was I stranded, but my home was open for public viewing. Laptop, phone, TV, documents — all just waiting for someone to say, "Thank you for your kindness."


I told myself, "One problem at a time." Low battery, stranded — I needed to get home first. So I opened Uber. Booked a cab. I watched the little car on the map come closer… closer… closer… then stop. And after 20 minutes — boom — "Ride Cancelled." I tried again. Same thing. Another 20 minutes. Cancelled again. A park ranger came over and asked if I got a cab. I said, "They keep cancelling halfway." And he goes, "Yeah, that happens here. They lose their network mid-way."


And that's when it hit me — If I didn't have network here, why would they? Like my brain finally realized how cause and effect works.


So now, I'm panicking. Alone. No network. Low battery. But I keep booking Ubers anyway. You know how they say "Work smart, not hard"? Yeah. I was proudly demonstrating "Work hard, not smart." Like one of those pigeons that keeps bumping into the same glass door again and again and never learns from mistakes. But this time I actually used some brain cells. As soon as the driver accepted, I texted him: "Hey, you'll lose signal on the way — download or memorize the route. If you make it, I'll pay extra." I was clearly desperate at this point and I wasn't trying to hide it in any way either. And miracle — he didn't cancel! I kept tracking him — until he reached the same cursed spot where the other cabs disappeared. It was my own personal Bermuda Triangle. After that point, I lost connection again. The ride wasn't cancelled, but I couldn't tell if he was still coming… or if he had given up on me like I had.


So I gave up. Cancelled the ride. Started downloading offline maps to walk back 8 miles home. No sidewalk. Just vibes and possible death. And because my phone battery was basically on life support, I started memorizing the directions like I was preparing for an exam. Like, "Okay… left at the weird tree… right at the creepy mailbox… straight until regret kicks in." If my phone died mid-way, I'd at least die knowing I was heading in the wrong direction.


Then suddenly I hear a ranger shout, "Miss! Your cab seems to have arrived!" I almost cried. I ran to the car, but nervously told the driver, "I cancelled the ride, but I'll still pay you extra like I promised." And he looks at me, dead serious, and says, "But… I came for you." This man — felt betrayed. He had no network, and didn't know I'd cancelled. He came all the way for me, and here I was, rejecting him like an emotional drama. I basically ghosted my Uber driver mid-journey. But he was so sweet, he still dropped me home. I paid him extra even though he refused — because let's be honest, that man deserves a national award.


I reached home. The unlocked home. Checked everything — laptop, TV, documents — all safe. Locked the door, finally sat down, I was hungry so ordered biryani because I felt I had earned it after all the things I had been through.



The goal was to lose weight. And I did — emotionally, spiritually, financially… Just not physically. But hey, I did gain something — Perspective. And maybe cholesterol.


At this point, I've accepted it. Delulu is, after all… my solulu.
