I had a 10 a.m. flight from San Jose to Chicago — simple, straightforward, adulting-friendly plan. I woke up at 6 a.m., reached the airport by 8 a.m., got my bag checked in, passed security like a champion, and reached the gate feeling… accomplished.


And that's when the universe said, "Awww, cute. Let me ruin that."


Act 1: The Pilot's Confession

The flight was delayed. Fine. Then the arriving plane finally parked, people got off… and the pilot himself walked out looking like a man who'd just survived a Marvel-level crisis. He told the ground staff: "There's a mechanical hydraulic issue. That's why landing took so long. Honestly, I was worried."


…Sir. Why. Why would you say that out loud. At the gate?? In public???


Anyway, the airline informed us it would take some time to investigate - 1–2 hours. Or more. Or never. Who knows. They offered options: Rebook another flight OR take the same flight… tomorrow


NO WAY was I going through security all over again the next day. That is a spiritual battle I fight only once per trip.


Act 2: The Mysterious SFO Option

They said they could put me on a direct flight from San Francisco. They'd even arrange a shuttle from San Jose airport. Perfect!


Except… half the people ahead of me refused like it was a cursed quest. I didn't ask why. I should've asked why. Instead I said, "Direct flight? Great!"


This was my first red flag. I ignored it like a pro.


Act 3: The Shuttle Ride

They put three of us into a car. San Jose → San Francisco normally takes 40 minutes. We reached it in less than 30 minutes. With traffic.


I don't know what dimension, shortcut, wormhole, or illegal Mario Kart track we took. But all of us were in the backseat silently debating our life choices, trying not to puke, and wondering whether the destination was:

  1. SFO Airport
  2. Heavens above
  3. Or the fiery depths below

We weren't judging — we've all sinned.

By the time we arrived, we were just grateful to still be among the living.


Act 4: Red Flag Realization

I rushed inside, checked my bag in again, did security again, and reached the gate. At this point I was starving. I ordered food… and while waiting:

THE FLIGHT GOT DELAYED. Because of bad weather. Because OF COURSE.


This is when I realized why everyone else refused the SFO option. They knew. THEY KNEW. But I? I was too optimistic. Too confident. Too… stupid.


It was 1 p.m. now. I had been traveling since 6 a.m. From Sunnyvale → San Jose → San Francisco. I had successfully made a reverse road trip, somehow traveling further AWAY from Chicago.


Act 5: The Emotional Drain Timeline

2 p.m. → delayed to 3

3 p.m. → delayed to 3:45

I aged approximately 6–10 years during this period.


My friend from Chicago texted asking what I wanted for dinner. I told her, very sincerely: "Just make rice and dal". I was at "don't-cook-too-much-I-might-not-survive-Californian-airports" level of despair.


She didn't know whether to laugh or worry. Me neither.


Act 6: The Miracle

At 3:45 p.m., the flight ACTUALLY BOARDED. I walked in like a survivor of four natural disasters. It finally took off. Chicago was no longer a myth. I reached at 11:30 p.m. local time — exhausted, hungry, and emotionally betrayed by California. But at least… At least I escaped the endless loop of airports.


I started in Sunnyvale trying to go to Chicago… and somehow ended up in San Francisco instead, before finally making it to Chicago. Forget flying — I was basically stuck in an airport-themed snakes-and-ladders game, and I kept landing on the snakes.
