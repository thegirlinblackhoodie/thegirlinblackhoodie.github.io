I was apartment hunting and had a viewing scheduled for late afternoon on a weekday. I booked a cab and went for the viewing. I liked the apartment, so I filled out the application and paid the application fee as well. I was super happy that my apartment hunting was finally coming to an end. In my head I had already mentally arranged my furniture.


I booked another cab, but decided to take it to the UPS near my home to pick up some packages. From there it would be just a 10-minute walk back home.


Once I reached UPS, I stood in line waiting for my turn. When I got to the counter, I said I had a package from Amazon and showed them the order details. They asked for my ID, which I had, so I showed them my driver’s license. They looked at it and said the name on the order was different.


I wasn’t sure what they meant. I checked my Amazon account again and realized the order was under my friend’s name with whom I share Amazon Prime. She lives in Chicago and I am in California. So unless UPS had suddenly started offering cross-country teleportation services, that wasn’t going to work.


I explained the situation but they said the only option was that I leave the package there, they would return it to Amazon marking that no one came to pick it up, and then I would get a refund once it reached back. Not the most efficient method, but apparently that was the only option.


I came out of UPS completely defeated and decided to walk back home. It was super hot, so I thought of wearing my sunglasses. I reached into my sling bag… and I couldn’t feel them. I could feel my phone, my keys, my wallet — but no glasses.


My heart sank. I could feel the emptiness and hollowness instantly. I cautiously touched my shirt collar hoping I had hung the glasses there… and nope. Nothing.


I rushed back into UPS and searched around but didn’t find them. I tried to retrace my steps and remember the last time I had them. I clearly remembered wearing them when I left the building where I had gone for the apartment viewing. Which meant the only logical answer was… I had left them in the Uber.


I checked the app to see what I could do. There was an option to contact the driver for lost items. I called him and he said he could bring it back but it would cost $20.


In my mind I was like — Those are Ray-Ban + Meta AI glasses! I was almost on the verge of crying and honestly $20 suddenly felt like the best deal of my life.


He said he could bring them back to the same location where he had dropped me. So I stood outside in the hot sun for about 30 minutes waiting.


When he finally came back and handed them to me, I thanked him like he had just returned my kid whom I had lost like a careless parent. He probably had no idea how expensive they were or how relieved I am.


I started walking back home thinking, well… at least something good came out of all this. I have an apartment almost finalized for me to move in next month.


And right at that exact moment I got an email.


My application was rejected because my income was too high and it was a low income rent-controlled apartment.


The apartment cost $2000. I can barely afford that and still live a comfortable life. I’m honestly not sure how someone with lower income is expected to afford it, but apparently I am both too poor for luxury apartments and too rich for rent-controlled ones. A truly elite financial position.


Nothing good came out of my little outing. All I managed to do was, lose some money, lose some peace and a lot of energy, and go through a full roller coaster of emotions… all in one afternoon.


And my friends, that’s just another perfectly normal day in my life!
