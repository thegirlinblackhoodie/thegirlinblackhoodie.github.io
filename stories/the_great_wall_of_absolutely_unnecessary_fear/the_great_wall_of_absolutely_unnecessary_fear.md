# The Great Wall of Absolutely Unnecessary Fear

Back in college, our daily commute involved a bus ride followed by a “shortcut” through a forest. I say shortcut, but it was really just a universally accepted illegal path that every student used just because it was a more fun route.

At the end of this forest adventure stood the college perimeter wall. Normally, this wall had a tiny opening—courtesy of past students—and some thoughtfully placed bricks acting as stairs. Two steps, a small hop, and boom, you’re inside campus, pretending this was all part of the design.

But one fine day, the college authorities woke up and chose violence. The stones were gone. The wall was rebuilt. And now it stood there in its full glory—taller than me, who proudly measures a whole 5'1" on a good posture day.

We found some huge bricks, stacked them like amateur architects, climbed up, and soon all three of us were sitting on top of the wall. My two friends—both taller and braver—jumped down effortlessly. One second they were there, the next they were inside campus, dusting themselves off like this was no big deal.

And then… there was me. I turned, put both my legs inside the campus, and just sat there. Frozen. Absolutely still.

Now logically, this was a simple wall jump. There was no slope. No cliff. No hill. No valley of doom waiting below. But my brain? My brain decided this was Mount Everest. Suddenly, I was convinced that the moment I jumped, I would roll. And keep rolling. Roll into another dimension. Roll into a documentary titled “Girl Who Rolled Forever.”

Why would I roll? There was nothing to roll down. But fear doesn’t care about logic. I refused to jump. Going back wasn’t an option either—same height, same jump, same imagined death. So I stayed there, sitting on the wall while my friends stared at me from below.

That’s when one of them had what can only be described as the most unnecessarily dramatic yet brilliant rescue plan in history. The plan was this:
They would stand on either side of me. 
I would place one leg on the right shoulder of one friend. The other leg on the left shoulder of the other friend. 
And then… slowly lower me down, like I was a priceless antique vase.

So there I was—legs on two shoulders, clinging to the wall, being carefully slid down like a human curtain, while my friends coordinated like professionals performing a hostage rescue. And it worked! My feet touched the ground. I survived. No rolling occurred.

And as I stood there, slightly traumatized, a lot embarrassed but fully alive, I realized I have the sweetest, most patient, most supportive best friends in the world. Everyone deserves friends who will lovingly overengineer a rescue plan — not because your life is at risk, but because your imagination clearly is.
