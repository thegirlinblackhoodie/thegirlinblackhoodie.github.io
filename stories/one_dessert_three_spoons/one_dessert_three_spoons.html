During COVID, all of India was on lockdown. We barely stepped out for almost a year. Life was just… indoors. Screens, family, and an occasional balcony moment.


Then slowly, things started calming down. People began meeting again—small, careful gatherings. So of course, I made a plan with my two besties.


We picked Hole in the Wall for brunch.But the moment we got there, reality hit us. A huge crowd. A long line. Zero social distancing. It didn’t feel safe anymore. So we did what any cautious-but-hungry people would do—we pivoted. We decided to walk to Chianti in Koramangala.


Now, Koramangala is usually chaotic. Traffic, people, noise—so packed you can barely walk. But that day? It was eerily empty. COVID fear had done what nothing else could.


And for once, luck was on our side. No reservation. No waiting. Just the three of us… and the entire restaurant. It felt unreal. The chef was cooking just for us. The waiters were attending only to us. It was borderline princess treatment. We ordered way too much food (obviously) and caught up on a year’s worth of life.


We were all on completely different paths.

  • I was preparing to move to the US for my master’s.
  • Friend A was deep into GATE prep.
  • Friend B was navigating medical life.

Different journeys, same table.


After lunch, we said our goodbyes and headed home, full—of food and emotions.


The next morning, things took a turn. Friend A messaged saying she had met another group of friends before us… and one of them had tested positive for COVID.


Which meant—she could’ve been carrying the virus when we met.


Friend B had just gotten her COVID vaccine right after our lunch. So now if she fell sick, we wouldn’t even know—is it the vaccine? Or COVID?


And me? I was just… there. Fine for now. Waiting.


Then it hit us. The dessert. One plate. Three spoons. No second thoughts. Like sisters — recklessly brushing off months of caution in one sweet bite!!!


We didn’t know whether to laugh at how stupid we’d been… or panic about what was coming next.


We asked Friend A to get tested. Friend B was confused. And me? I had already accepted our fate.


After all that panic, waiting, overthinking…

  • Friend A tests negative.
  • Friend B just has normal vaccine symptoms.
  • I never fell sick.

For a few days, every cough felt like a countdown. Every message notification made our hearts race. But in the end, it passed—like everything else that year. Quietly, and with just enough fear to make it unforgettable.
