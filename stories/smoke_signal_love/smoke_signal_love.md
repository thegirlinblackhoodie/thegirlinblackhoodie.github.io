# Smoke Signals of Love

My sister is pregnant and will be on maternity leave for about six months before coming back and rejoining work. Naturally, I miss her. So I sent her one WhatsApp message. She replied. She said she was bored. And that’s when I decided it was my personal responsibility to become her full-time entertainment system. I took this role very seriously.
I started texting her constantly—office gossip, random life updates and dramatic retellings of my workday. I recommended TV series she might like, blog posts she should read, and very specifically, my own new blog posts that she must read.
I was texting her while grocery shopping. Texting her while unpacking groceries. Texting her while planning my meals for the week. Eventually, I started cooking. 
Now here’s where things began to spiral. Cooking and texting don’t mix very well—especially when your hands are oily, spicy, or generally unfit to touch a phone. After struggling for a bit, I made what I thought was a reasonable decision: Pause cooking. Continue entertaining sister. So I left the kitchen, sat on my couch in the living room (the kitchen is right behind it), and resumed my duty.
We drifted through everything—life, work, my quiet crushes, and the unanswered question of how one finds love at all. She offered advice the way older souls do. Time passed. At some point—maybe 30 minutes later—I thought, Okay, enough bonding. Let’s cook now.
I stood up. Turned around. And saw SMOKE. A LOT of smoke. The kitchen was completely filled with it. And that’s when my brain finally rebooted and reminded me:
I HAD LEFT A PAN WITH OIL ON THE STOVE WITH THE STOVE ON
I ran. Switched it off immediately. Opened all the windows. Opened all the doors. Started aggressively inviting fresh air into my house like, PLEASE, SAVE ME.
And then—BEEP. BEEP. BEEP.
The fire alarm went off.
Not when the danger began.
 Not when intervention might have been useful.
It went off only after the situation had escalated so visibly that anyone with functioning eyes already knew.

And to make things worse, it wasn’t just ringing. There was a woman’s voice saying “Fire. Fire. Fire.” As if I needed clarification.
Now began my journey of How To Stop a Fire Alarm When You Are Panicking.
First attempt:
I took a towel and started rotating it like a fan directly under the alarm, hoping to push the smoke away. Did it work? Absolutely not.
Second attempt:
I grabbed a broomstick and tried poking the snooze button like I was playing some high-stakes arcade game. Missed it completely.
Final attempt:
Standing on something seemed like the answer. I reached for a tiny stool, barely a foot high. At 5'1, it did nothing except quietly mock my ambition. Then I pushed my work chair out of my room. Important detail: This chair has wheels.
There was no one to hold it. There was no safety plan. I climbed onto the moving chair, balanced myself like I was auditioning for a circus, reached up, and finally pressed the snooze button. The alarm stopped. The smoke slowly cleared. The house survived. I survived.
And my sister? She was still on WhatsApp. Very entertained.

Moral of the story:
I love my sister. And sometimes love looks like chaos, smoke, and a very loud alarm.
I’d help her again without hesitation—just after ensuring the stove is off.
